§ 2 — DERIVATION OF THE EQUATIONS OF MOTION

From bases to Lagrange to a matrix model

2.1  Define the origin and the bases

Origin O sits on the motor axis. The bases are built by two successive rotations — first \theta about the fixed vertical, then \varphi about the (now rotated) arm axis:

\bas{E} : \text{ fixed at } O; \qquad \bas{e}' : \bas{E} \text{ rotated by } \theta \text{ about } \vb{E}{}_3; \qquad \bas{e} : \bas{e}' \text{ rotated by } \varphi \text{ about } \vb{e}'{}_1

The inertias of the two bars, about their respective reference points, are

{}^B\mb{J}^O{}_{\bas{e}'} = \mathrm{diag}({}^B J{}_1, {}^B J{}_2, {}^B J{}_3), \qquad {}^A\mb{J}^S{}_{\bas{e}} = \mathrm{diag}({}^A J{}_1, {}^A J{}_2, {}^A J{}_3)

and the total angular velocity of bar A, expressed in \bas{e}, together with the cross products needed later, are

\bomega = \dot{\theta}\,\vb{e}'{}_3 + \dot{\varphi}\,\vb{e}{}_1 = \dot{\varphi}\,\vb{e}{}_1 + \dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_2 + \dot{\theta}\cos\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_3
\bomega \times \vb{e}{}_1 = -\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_3 + \dot{\theta}\cos\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_2, \qquad \bomega \times \vb{e}{}_3 = -\dot{\varphi}\,\vb{e}{}_2 + \dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_1

2.2  Kinematics

For the centre of mass of bar B (the arm), with \vb{e}{}_1 = \vb{e}'{}_1:

\vb{r}{}_B = \frac{l{}_B}{2}\vb{e}'{}_1 = \frac{l{}_B}{2}\vb{e}{}_1, \qquad \dot{\vb{r}}{}_B = \frac{l{}_B}{2}\dot{\vb{e}}{}_1 = \frac{l{}_B}{2}\left(-\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_3 + \dot{\theta}\cos\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_2\right)
\dot{\vb{r}}{}_B \cdot \dot{\vb{r}}{}_B = \frac{l{}_B^2}{4}\,\dot{\theta}^2, \qquad {}^B\bomega = \dot{\theta}\,\vb{e}'{}_3, \qquad {}^B\bomega \cdot {}^B\mb{J} \cdot {}^B\bomega = {}^B J{}_3\,\dot{\theta}^2

For the centre of mass of bar A (the pendulum):

\vb{r}{}_A = \vb{r}{}_B + \frac{l{}_A}{2}\vb{e}{}_3 = l{}_B\,\vb{e}{}_1 + \frac{l{}_A}{2}\vb{e}{}_3
\dot{\vb{r}}{}_A = \frac{l{}_A}{2}\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_1 + \left[-\frac{l{}_A}{2}\dot{\varphi} + l{}_B\dot{\theta}\cos\varphi\right]\vb{e}{}_2 - l{}_B\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_3
\dot{\vb{r}}{}_A \cdot \dot{\vb{r}}{}_A = \frac{l{}_A^2}{4}\left[\dot{\theta}^2\sin^2\varphi + \dot{\varphi}^2\right] + l{}_B^2\dot{\theta}^2 - l{}_A l{}_B\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\varphi}\cos\varphi
{}^A\bomega = \dot{\varphi}\,\vb{e}{}_1 + \dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_2 + \dot{\theta}\cos\varphi\,\vb{e}{}_3, \qquad {}^A\bomega \cdot {}^A\mb{J} \cdot {}^A\bomega = {}^A J{}_1\dot{\varphi}^2 + {}^A J{}_2\dot{\theta}^2\sin^2\varphi + {}^A J{}_3\dot{\theta}^2\cos^2\varphi

The arm is not inert: as the pendulum swings, angular momentum about the vertical is shared, so the arm rotates in opposition — the effective pendulum inertia about the hanging equilibrium is the Schur complement J{}_\varphi - L^2/J{}_\theta, not J{}_\varphi. In the simulator, release the pendulum from a small tilt with control off and watch the arm counter-rotate in step.

2.3  Forces and energy

The only conservative force is gravity on bar A — bar B never changes height, so it drops out of the potential. The non-conservative actuator moment is \vb{M} = M(t)\vb{E}{}_3; constraint forces do no work.

T = \frac{1}{2}m{}_A\,\dot{\vb{r}}{}_A\cdot\dot{\vb{r}}{}_A + \frac{1}{2}m{}_B\,\dot{\vb{r}}{}_B\cdot\dot{\vb{r}}{}_B + \frac{1}{2}\,{}^A\bomega\cdot{}^A\mb{J}\cdot{}^A\bomega + \frac{1}{2}\,{}^B\bomega\cdot{}^B\mb{J}\cdot{}^B\bomega
T = \frac{1}{2}m{}_A\!\left(\frac{l{}_A^2}{4}\left[\dot{\theta}^2\sin^2\varphi + \dot{\varphi}^2\right] + l{}_B^2\dot{\theta}^2 - l{}_A l{}_B\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\varphi}\cos\varphi\right) + \frac{1}{2}m{}_B\frac{l{}_B^2}{4}\dot{\theta}^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left({}^A J{}_1\dot{\varphi}^2 + \left[{}^A J{}_2\sin^2\varphi + {}^A J{}_3\cos^2\varphi + {}^B J{}_3\right]\dot{\theta}^2\right)
V = m{}_A g\,\vb{E}{}_3 \cdot \vb{r}{}_A = m{}_A g \frac{l{}_A}{2}\cos\varphi

2.4  Equations of motion (Lagrange)

\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial T}{\partial \dot{q}} - \frac{\partial T}{\partial q} + \frac{\partial V}{\partial q} = \sum \left(\vb{F}^{nc}\cdot\frac{\partial \vb{r}}{\partial q} + \mb{M}^{nc}\cdot\frac{\partial \bomega}{\partial \dot{q}}\right)

For q = \theta, the generalized force of the actuator is M(t), and one obtains

\left[J{}_\theta + B\sin^2\varphi\right]\ddot{\theta} + 2B\sin\varphi\cos\varphi\,\dot{\theta}\dot{\varphi} - L\cos\varphi\,\ddot{\varphi} + L\sin\varphi\,\dot{\varphi}^2 = M(t)

For q = \varphi, the actuator does no work in that direction and the gravity torque appears:

J{}_\varphi\ddot{\varphi} - L\cos\varphi\,\ddot{\theta} - B\dot{\theta}^2\sin\varphi\cos\varphi = m{}_A g \frac{l{}_A}{2}\sin\varphi

Two unknowns, two equations — the model is complete. Rewriting in matrix form,

\mb{M}\,\ddot{\vb{q}} + \mb{C}\,\dot{\vb{q}} = \vb{\uptau}, \qquad \vb{q} = \begin{bmatrix} \theta \\ \varphi \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \vb{\uptau} = \begin{bmatrix} M(t) \\ m{}_A g \frac{l{}_A}{2}\sin\varphi \end{bmatrix}

with the shorthand terms (all constant for a given build):

J{}_\theta = m{}_A l{}_B^2 + m{}_B\frac{l{}_B^2}{4} + {}^B J{}_3 + {}^A J{}_3 \quad \text{(constant base inertia)}
J{}_\varphi = m{}_A\frac{l{}_A^2}{4} + {}^A J{}_1 \quad \text{(constant pendulum inertia)}, \qquad B = m{}_A\frac{l{}_A^2}{4} + {}^A J{}_2 - {}^A J{}_3 \quad \text{(inertia variation amplitude)}
L = \frac{l{}_A l{}_B}{2}m{}_A \quad \text{(coupling magnitude)}

and the mass matrix \mb{M}(\vb{q}) and Christoffel matrix \mb{C}(\vb{q},\dot{\vb{q}}):

\mb{M} = \begin{bmatrix} J{}_\theta + B\sin^2\varphi & -L\cos\varphi \\ -L\cos\varphi & J{}_\varphi \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mb{C} = \begin{bmatrix} B\dot{\varphi}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi & B\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi + L\dot{\varphi}\sin\varphi \\ -B\dot{\theta}\sin\varphi\cos\varphi & 0 \end{bmatrix}

The simulator integrates this exact nonlinear model with RK4 at 1 kHz. A cross-check against the raw Lagrange form (2.4) agrees to machine precision, and with M(t) = 0 the mechanical energy E = T + V drifts by less than 10^{-4} relative over 10 s.

2.5  Linearization about upright & LQR balance

Around the upright equilibrium (\varphi, \dot{\varphi}, \theta, \dot{\theta}) = (0,0,0,0) the nonlinear model collapses to the linear state-space form \dot{\vb{x}} = \mb{A}\vb{x} + \mb{B}M with \vb{x} = [\varphi,\ \theta,\ \dot{\varphi},\ \dot{\theta}]^{\mathsf T}:

\mb{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & 1 \\ \dfrac{m{}_A g l{}_A}{2}\dfrac{J{}_\theta}{\Delta} & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ \dfrac{m{}_A g l{}_A}{2}\dfrac{L}{\Delta} & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mb{B} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ \dfrac{L}{\Delta} \\ \dfrac{J{}_\varphi}{\Delta} \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \Delta = J{}_\theta J{}_\varphi - L^2

Note the structure: \varphi feeds both rows (gravity pulls the pendulum over; the lean also torques the arm), \theta is a pure integrator, and the torque enters both accelerations through the coupling. The pair is controllable (rank of the controllability matrix is 4).

LQR chooses M = -\mb{K}\vb{x} to minimize

J = \int_0^\infty \left(\vb{x}^{\mathsf T}\mb{Q}\vb{x} + R\,M^2\right) dt
\mb{K} = R^{-1}\mb{B}^{\mathsf T}\mb{P}, \qquad \mb{A}^{\mathsf T}\mb{P} + \mb{P}\mb{A} - \mb{P}\mb{B}R^{-1}\mb{B}^{\mathsf T}\mb{P} + \mb{Q} = \mb{0}

The Riccati equation is solved at runtime by iterating its discrete-time equivalent at the 200 Hz control rate, so the gains adapt instantly when you change \mb{Q}, R or the hardware in the simulator. The default weights \mb{Q} = \mathrm{diag}(300,\ 25,\ 5,\ 1.5), R = 0.012 place the closed-loop pendulum poles well inside the unit circle with a healthy torque margin.

2.6  Swing-up: energy control

LQR only works near the upright. Starting from hanging (\varphi = \pi) we pump energy into the pendulum with the arm, using the pendulum energy with datum at the upright:

E = \frac{1}{2}J{}_\varphi\dot{\varphi}^2 + m{}_A g \frac{l{}_A}{2}\left(\cos\varphi - 1\right) \qquad \Rightarrow \qquad E_{ref} = 0
M_{swing}(t) = -k{}_E \cdot E \cdot \operatorname{sgn}\left(\dot{\varphi}\cos\varphi\right)

Sign note. With the basis convention above, \dot E \propto \dot{\varphi}\cos\varphi \cdot M to leading order, so the minus sign makes E \to E_{ref}: when the pendulum lacks energy the arm accelerates in the direction of the pendulum's swing — exactly the behaviour described in the project notes. (The literal sign printed there would remove energy; the verbal description pins the correct one.) The simulator defaults to the saturated bang–bang form M = \pm M_{max} for a fast, robust swing-up, with optional arm-velocity damping to stop the arm winding up.
